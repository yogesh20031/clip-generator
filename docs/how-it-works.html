From a YouTube link to a finished reel

You paste a link. About five minutes later there's a vertical, captioned clip in your folder, along with its title, hook and hashtags for each platform. This page walks through every step: what happens, which tool does it, and where Claude comes in.

Each stop, in plain words

What actually happens

1

Get the video

The link goes to yt-dlp, which downloads the best-quality version as source.mp4 in a new folder named after the video. ffprobe then reads its length, size and frame rate. A file already on your Mac works too, and gets no download.

yt-dlpffprobeingest/
2

Listen & watch

Four checks run over the whole video. The results are saved in analysis.json, so a second run on the same video skips this step.

Words: Whisper turns the speech into text with a timestamp on every word. On your M4 it runs on the GPU through mlx-whisper. Lines that Whisper made up over music or engine noise get thrown out. Sound: loudness is measured 4 times a second, to spot shouts, laughs and bangs. Scenes: PySceneDetect marks every camera cut. Motion: OpenCV compares frames 4 times a second to see how much is moving, and where on screen.

mlx-whisperffmpegPySceneDetectOpenCVanalysis/
3

Find the story

The video is cut into stories, meaning one challenge, one mission or one round each. Story breaks are found from phrases like "for our next challenge", a change of topic or of names, long pauses, and camera cuts. Inside each story the code labels the parts: who, what's at stake, the payoff (the result) and the reaction. Then it builds the edit: keep what a stranger needs to follow along, cut the filler and any pause over half a second.

Every edit gets a score from the niche profile (profiles/gta6.yaml, profiles/mrbeast.yaml). The profile says what counts in that niche: crashes and chases for GTA, money and wins for MrBeast. The best edits that don't overlap become the clips. A video with no talking, like raw gameplay, gets one continuous action stretch instead.

scoring/stories.pyscoring/edits.pyscoring/scorer.py
4

Cut & frame

ffmpeg cuts each kept piece and crops the wide frame to a vertical one. The crop follows faces, or the moving car, from moment to moment. Then it joins the pieces with tiny 15 ms fades so there are no clicks at the cuts. It burns in big word-by-word captions, shows the hook card for the first 3.5 seconds, and sets the loudness to −14 LUFS, the level the platforms play at. A thumbnail is saved too.

ffmpegPillowrender/
5

Write the words

Templates from the niche profile fill in a hook, title, description and hashtags for YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram Reels and Facebook Reels, each within that platform's limits. If the source is Creative Commons, a credit line for the creator goes at the end. Everything is written to clip_01.txt so you can copy and paste it.

metadata/templates.pymetadata/platforms.py
6

Claude checks it

The automatic pick is a good first draft, but not always the best clip. Claude reads the story map, looks at small grids of frames from the video, and either confirms the pick or cuts a better one with render_edit. Then it writes the hook and captions about what's actually on screen, using set_clip_metadata.

story_maprender_editset_clip_metadata

Four tracks over the same timeline

After step 2, every second of the video has four readings. Peaks that line up across the tracks are where something happened.

Figure 3 · one story, one reel

Keep the story, cut the waiting

This is the rule you set: a reel holds one complete story and never stops in the middle. A random 60-second window usually misses the setup or the result. A story edit keeps every part a stranger needs and drops the filler between them. Real example: the Ronaldo $1M challenge became clip_02.

Figure 4 · wide to tall

The vertical frame follows the action

YouTube videos are wide (16:9) and reels are tall (9:16), so about two-thirds of each frame has to go. The crop window slides left and right to stay on whatever matters: a face when someone is talking, or the moving car in gameplay.

Figure 5 · where Claude fits

Your Mac does the heavy lifting. Claude does the judgement.

Your Mac (the repo's code)

  • Downloads the video
  • Runs Whisper for the transcript, on the M4's GPU
  • Measures loudness, motion and camera cuts
  • Finds the stories and scores them
  • Cuts, crops, captions and exports the MP4
  • Fills in template titles and hashtags

No API key and no upload. The video file never leaves your computer.

Claude (in this chat)

  • Built the repo and fixes it when a clip comes out wrong. For example: made-up captions on gameplay, or GTA 5 labelled as GTA 6.
  • Runs it when you paste a link, through the terminal command or the MCP tools.
  • Checks the pick: reads the story map and looks at small frame grids, then chooses the complete story.
  • Writes the words: a hook and captions about what's really in the clip.

Claude sees text (the transcript and story map) and the small preview images it makes. It never gets the full video.

The two ways Claude can drive the repo

Terminal commands (what happens today): Claude runs uv run viral-clipper … commands for you.

MCP tools (after you register the server once): Claude calls the tools directly: clip_video, story_map, render_edit, cut_clip, set_clip_metadata, list_outputs. MCP is the standard way to give Claude new tools. server.py is the file that exposes them.

The commands behind it

# automatic: download, analyze, and cut the best N clips
uv run viral-clipper clip "https://youtube.com/watch?v=…" --niche gta6 --num-clips 1

# list the stories with numbered lines, so you can choose one yourself
uv run viral-clipper stories "https://youtube.com/watch?v=…" --niche gta6

# cut exactly the pieces you chose (seconds or mm:ss), with your own hook
uv run viral-clipper edit "https://youtube.com/watch?v=…" --seg 147.5-166.7 --hook "Rocket car vs the BIGGEST mega ramp"

You don't have to type these. Paste a link in the chat and Claude runs them.

Rights  Clips of someone else's video are theirs. Creative Commons sources get the credit line automatically. For anything else, add your own voice or reaction before posting: the platforms show plain re-uploads to fewer people.
